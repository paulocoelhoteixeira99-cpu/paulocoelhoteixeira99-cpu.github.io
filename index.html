Case Study

Voucher & Reimbursement Policy Analytics

Everyone was cutting vouchers to save money. I proved with data that it was costing the company revenue, and reversed the strategy.

The Counter-Intuitive Discovery

Over 18 months, the company had revised its voucher policy 6 times, always in the same direction: reducing voucher values to cut costs. On the surface, the math worked. But I started asking: "at what point does reducing vouchers stop being positive?"

By cross-referencing data from CX, finance, marketplace, and legal teams, I discovered that cutting vouchers was actually hurting customer recurrence. Customers who received adequate compensation after incidents came back and spent more. Those who received minimal vouchers often didn't return at all.

I created the ACPU metric (Average Cash Per User) to prove it: ACPU = average ticket x conversion rate. This single metric captured the full picture, measuring not just cost but the revenue generated per user over time. The data showed that higher vouchers (30%) often produced better ACPU than lower ones (15%), because the recurrence lift more than offset the higher upfront cost.

What I Built

AI Voice Agent System

Built the complete call automation pipeline: scheduled outbound calls via VAPI with time-zone validation, dual VoIP routing, GPT-powered post-call analysis, and automatic outcome classification. 26,728 calls executed with 52.9% contact rate.

Credit-Based Billing Engine

Designed the full SaaS monetization layer from scratch. Each operational action (call, SMS, email) fires a PostgreSQL trigger that: (1) looks up the client's negotiated markup percentage, (2) applies it to the raw cost, (3) converts the marked-up value to internal credits (100 credits = $1), (4) debits the client's balance, and (5) logs the full transaction with audit metadata. Stripe handles credit purchases via a $0.01/unit product, enabling seamless top-ups from $10 to $1,000. The system processed 38,280 transactions across 4 clients with zero manual intervention.

Client Portal

Built a real-time client portal with Supabase Auth: executive dashboard with KPIs (leads, contact rate, sales, revenue, conversion vs target), billing page with credit packages, auto-recharge config, payment history, and Stripe card management.

Lead Ingestion & Validation Pipeline

Engineered per-client webhooks that receive abandoned cart payloads, normalize the data, and validate phone numbers via Twilio Lookup API before entering the pipeline. Saved costs by discarding 539 invalid phones that would have wasted SMS and call credits.

Multi-Channel Recovery Cadence

Orchestrated a 3-channel recovery flow: AI calls for high-intent leads, a 5-step SMS drip sequence for re-engagement, and email follow-ups via ActiveCampaign. Calls convert at ~50%, and 45% of sales close on the first attempt.

Dual Analytics Layer

Created both an internal Metabase dashboard (volumetrics, contact rates, conversion by source/checkout/classification) and a client-facing portal dashboard with real-time metrics. Tracked across 3 checkout platforms (Digistore, CartPanda, Spiffy/Stripe).

B2B Group Conclusion Automation

Reduced manual group conclusions from 18.2% to 3.4%, cutting daily workload by 84% and eliminating the need for a dedicated analyst

Overview

In B2B operations at a major Brazilian bus transportation company, every travel group with an incident or report got "stuck" and required manual review by the BX team before closure. Analysts had to evaluate each case against the Reimbursement Policy to determine if passengers qualified for vouchers or cash reimbursement. However, many incident groups didn't qualify for any treatment yet still consumed the same analyst time as complex cases. By June, these manual conclusions represented ~20% of all company travel groups. This was one of the team's heaviest demands, pulling consultants away from their core mission of supporting drivers and partners.

Key Metrics

183 to 29 Daily manual conclusions (before/after)
18.2% to 3.4% Manual conclusion rate
-84% Reduction in daily workload
OKR: 16% Target was 16%, achieved 3.4%

The Problem

From January to June, the team averaged 183 manual conclusions per day, requiring 1 dedicated analyst full-time plus the rest of the B2B team helping in parallel. At full capacity, 2 full-time consultants would be needed just for group conclusions. The situation worsened in Q4 holiday peaks, where 4 to 5 consultants had to be pulled from their regular duties to handle the surge. The team was scaling headcount to handle volume rather than eliminating the root cause.

What I Built

Volume & Impact Analysis

Quantified the 183/day bottleneck, proved the 2-FTE equivalent cost, and modeled Q4 peak-season impact (4-5 consultants diverted). This data was the foundation for leadership buy-in on the automation investment.

Policy-to-Automation Mapping

Worked with the internal controls team to map every incident category (delays, cancellations, route changes, downgrades, no-shows) against the Reimbursement Policy, identifying which groups could be auto-resolved vs. which required human review.

Decision Tree Specification

Delivered the complete automation logic: auto-close groups that don't qualify for treatment, auto-process clear-cut reimbursements, and route only complex multi-incident cases to human review.
